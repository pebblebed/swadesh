Swadesh working notes · on representation

A sieve,
not a target

ASJP's coarse alphabet gives a uniform layer across every list and a free external yardstick — but a 41-way softmax would optimize away the vowels we set out to model.

The ASJP maneuver · for the Rosetta Swadesh corpus

Two good questions, and the second contains the whole decision. Is the representation a stack — raw → IPA → ASJP for the lists we can convert, and raw → ASJP directly for the rest? Yes, essentially. And does the model then carry a 41-way ASJP-only softmax? It can — and for this project it shouldn't. ASJP is most brutal exactly where our stated goal is most demanding. Let me lay out what it is, what it discards, how the stack falls out, and where I'd stop the softmax.

What ASJP is forThe Automated Similarity Judgment Program (Brown, Holman, Wichmann et al.) was built to compare thousands of languages from short core lists, mechanically. Coarseness is a feature: it makes transcription differences vanish.

ASJPcode is a deliberately blunt, ASCII-only phonetic alphabet: 41 classes — seven vowels, the rest consonants. It exists to be robust, not faithful. Paired with normalized Levenshtein distance (LDND), it powers a database of ~40-item lists for some seven thousand doculects, with published distances and trees. That database is the prize we get for free if we can speak its language.

The inventoryForty-one shadows of the IPA chart

Fine enough to keep a consonant skeleton; far too coarse to keep a vowel system.

The collapseWhat the sieve drops

The analytically important thing is not what ASJP keeps — it's what falls through.

The mismatch in one lineOur goal is to model vocalic sound change. ASJP keeps 7 vowel buckets and discards length, nasalization, and tone — it is lossy precisely on the axis we care about.

The stackTwo paths into one layer — yes

Your picture is right, and from our side it is nearly free.

So ASJP earns its keep two ways at once: it gives a single representation that survives the IPA-vs-Americanist-vs-orthography heterogeneity, and it lets nothing be excluded from training — the data-poor tail still has a row. The feature layer is the superset; ASJP is the lossy view of it that everyone can share.

The forkWhy not a 41-way ASJP softmax

Clean, uniform, well-precedented — and the wrong primary target here.

The trap

An ASJP-only 41-way softmax trains the decoder to reproduce a representation that has already thrown away length, nasalization, tone, and most vowel quality. You would be optimizing away the exact signal the project exists to capture. The result is a serviceable cognate / relatedness model and a useless model of vocalic change.

The instinct behind it is sound — you want one output everyone can supervise. But the cure isn't to coarsen the target to the worst-covered list; it's to let the output degrade gracefully by list quality. Keep ASJP, but as the backbone, not the ceiling.

Three jobsWhat ASJP is genuinely good for

None of them is "be the output."

1

Uniform fallback coverage.

The representation the data-poor tail actually has. With it, the 40 stubborn lists and every low-confidence record still contribute to z_language instead of being dropped.

2

External anchor.

Reproduce LDND and check our learned z_language geometry against the published ASJP database and Glottolog trees. Free ground truth — a sanity rail for the whole relatedness claim.

3

A coarse backbone head.

A robust, always-supervised class prediction that regularizes the fine heads and gives the model a stable scaffold to hang vowel detail on — not the whole output.

The designFactored, not flat

One coarse class everyone shares × fine vowel heads where the source can support them.

This is the move: build the IPA→ASJP collapse now (it costs little, hands us coverage and the external anchor immediately), write the easy orthography→ASJP rules for the last 40, but keep the segment-feature tensor as the decoder's primary target. ASJP becomes the backbone class in a factored head — never a 41-way bottleneck the vowels have to squeeze through.

VerdictSpeak ASJP; don't think in it

In short

Yes to the stack: raw → IPA → features → ASJP for the 1,186, raw → simple rules → ASJP for the 40, into one uniform layer. ASJP is a lossy projection of the feature work already done, and a cheap rescue for the tail.

No to the 41-way ASJP-only softmax as the primary target — it discards length, nasalization, tone, and most vowel quality, which is the precise signal a vocalic-change model must keep. Make ASJP the coarse backbone of a factored output (class × masked fine vowel heads) and the external yardstick for z_language; let the segment-feature tensor be what the decoder actually emits.

Speak ASJP fluently — for coverage and for grounding against seven thousand catalogued doculects. Just don't let the model think in it.
