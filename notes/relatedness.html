Swadesh working notes · on method

Coherence,
not closeness

Measuring how two languages are related by the systematicity of the map between their word lists — where the idea sits in linguistics, and where the naïve version breaks.

Comparative method, computationally · for the Rosetta Swadesh corpus

Stepping back from transcription mechanics is the right instinct — and the good news is that your intuition isn't merely reasonable; it is the literal foundation of historical linguistics. Relatedness measured by the regularity of the correspondence, not the smallness of the difference, is exactly the principle the field was built on. Let me place the idea, then push on it.

The thesisTwo languages are sisters not when their words look alike but when a small, consistent set of correspondences maps one onto the other. Surface similarity is the thing the method warns you against.

What you're describing is regular sound correspondence — the Neogrammarian principle (Brugmann & Osthoff, 1870s): sound laws are exceptionless. The textbook instances are your "simple coherent rules." Grimm's Law (1822): Proto-Indo-European voiceless stops became Germanic voiceless fricatives — Latin pater : English father, piscis : fish. That is precisely "shift this natural class." Verner's Law (1876) then rescued Grimm's exceptions by adding a single conditioning factor — accent — which is the most important caveat for your scheme, and we return to it below.

The worked exampleThe map can be large and still be regular

Polynesian is the cleanest case there is — and your own example nails it.

The whole pointThe gap between "far in Levenshtein" and "trivially regular" is the signal you want to measure. ASJP's normalized edit distance lives on the wrong side of that gap.

Run a raw edit-distance over those pairs and you get a non-trivial number; the words genuinely differ. But the same substitution k ↔ t recurs, and that recurrence — not the magnitude of the difference — is the evidence of common descent. This is why the comparative method does not use surface resemblance: phonetic similarity is chance, nursery words, and loans; recurrent correspondence is descent. Your framing — "we don't care if the words are far apart, as long as the edit procedure is consistent" — is a clean restatement of the discipline's central distinction.

List · LexStat

Wettig · Yangarber
Helsinki "Etymon" project. MDL models of context-conditioned correspondences in Uralic — the deepest match to what you actually want, conditioning included.
Bouchard-Côté ’13
Bayesian sound change on a phylogeny. Stochastic transducers as the change process, reconstructing Proto-Austronesian. Your "coherent rule" is their parameterized process.
Ristad-Yianilos ’98
Learning string edit distance. The ML substrate: a weighted transducer fit to data — "the rule" as a learned object.

So in essence — not new. But the framing you are drawn to is under-exploited: relatedness as the coverage of the single best low-complexity ruleset, with the ruleset itself as the interpretable output. Distance methods discard the rule; cognate-detectors emit cognate sets, not a readable "here are the five rules that explain 60% of the list." That legibility is worth keeping.

The critiqueWhere the naïve form breaks

Four failure modes — each with a principled repair that converges on the literature above.

1

Unbounded rules explain everything.

"% explained by the optimal rule" is degenerate without a complexity penalty — one rule per word reaches 100%. The fix is your own word, "mindless": this is Minimum Description Length. Relatedness ∝ L(rules) + L(data | rules) against encoding the lists independently. The real signal is coverage per unit of rule complexity.

2

Rules must be conditioned — but not too much.

Verner's Law shows genuine laws often need context; without it you under-explain true sisters. With rich context you overfit. The MDL penalty arbitrates — and your feature layer is the right complexity prior: "voice the fricatives anterior to velar" is cheap (one feature-flip on a natural class); an arbitrary nine-phoneme set is expensive. Features give the cost function over rules — not, as "vector displacement" implies, the geometry. Correspondences are relations on segments, often many-to-one.

3

Direction and mergers.

Mergers are non-invertible: if B collapsed *s, *θ → s, you cannot recover θ going back, so coverage(A→B) ≠ coverage(B→A). The comparative method dodges this with undirected correspondences — A's segment ↔ B's segment in context, both pointing at a proto-form — rather than a directional rewrite. Recast your "rule A→B" as a correspondence set and the asymmetry largely dissolves.

4

No null model, no meaning.

Inventory overlap, nursery forms (mama), onomatopoeia and loans give even unrelated pairs nonzero coverage from the identity rule. Relatedness must be excess coverage over a permutation baseline (Kessler). And there is a ceiling: even perfect sisters miss 100% because Swadesh lists undergo lexical replacement — same-meaning ≠ cognate. Your "unexplained fraction" absorbs that cleanly, which is a virtue, not a bug.

One more hazardBorrowing can be highly regular (systematic loan adaptation), so high coverage alone won't separate inheritance from contact. Stratifying by Swadesh stability rank — basic vocabulary resists borrowing — is the usual lever.

None of these sink the idea. Each one bends the naïve "% explained by the best rule" into something the field already recognizes — a description-length objective over feature-natural correspondences, scored against a permutation null — at which point it is principled rather than ad hoc.

VerdictSound at the core, fresh at the edge

In short

The intuition is the discipline's bedrock, and the distinction you draw from edit distance is exactly the right one. The naïve form — unconditioned global rule, raw percentage, directional, no baseline — has well-known failure modes, and fixing them converges on Wettig–Yangarber and LexStat.

What stays distinctive is the packaging: a minimum-description-length coverage by a feature-natural correspondence set, scored against a permutation null, and reported as a human-readable ruleset. Most pipelines compute a number and discard the explanation; keeping the explanation is the worthwhile, under-built move.

And you are unusually well-positioned — ~244k records now carry a per-segment feature layer, which is precisely the substrate for defining rule cost over natural classes. Polynesian is the ideal first testbed: small inventories, clean ground truth, and a known answer to check against — does the metric recover k : t and the *f, *s → h merger?
