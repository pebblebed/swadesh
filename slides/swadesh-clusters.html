The question

Are the extensions coherent — or just noise?

After folding 22 surface synonyms onto the canonical 207, 86 non-canonical concepts remain. The test for a real "cluster" is not that words are semantically related, but that they co-occur in the same lists — i.e. they came from the same questionnaire.

Two measures drive every slide that follows:

measure 1

Jaccard overlap

How often two glosses appear together: |A∩B| / |A∪B|. High ⇒ they travel as a pair.

measure 2

Regional lift

P(gloss | template lists) ÷ P(gloss | the rest). High ⇒ the gloss is geographically pinned.

The key finding

It's mostly one instrument — used worldwide.

Blocks ②–⑤ are not independent. 265 non-Sahul lists with ≥8 extensions share the same extras — and they span every corner of the map. The Sahul fauna is simply the regional page of a single extended wordlist.

The exceptions

Not clusters — free-floating substitutes

A few high-frequency extras spread evenly everywhere (lift ≈ 1, Jaccard ≈ 0.28 with everything). They're individual lexical choices, not templates:

  • claw — 387 lists (144 Sahul / 243 not). The Swadesh-100 word standing in for the 207-list's fingernail.
  • you — 549 lists. A bare 2nd person where the 207 splits singular / plural.
  • house, fly (n.), wood — the universal "everyone adds these" extensions.

Takeaways

  • One dominant extended questionnaire (~200 items) underlies most large lists, decomposing into tight body-part / kinship / time / weather sub-blocks.
  • One regional add-on — the Sahul fauna page (449 lists, Australia + New Guinea).
  • One genuinely grammatical cluster — dual/clusivity pronouns, cross-family (Khoisan → Caucasian → Ainu).
  • A handful of free-floating substitutes (claw, you) that belong to no template.
